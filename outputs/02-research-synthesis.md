# 02 Research synthesis

Summary (signals from 4 synthetic interviews and 15 survey answers, not proportions):
- **Customers.** The customers interviewed read "unremarkable" as "healthy", struggle with the German, jargon-heavy report, and hear nothing after it. Both say they need a prompt to return. They disagree on who should send it: their optician, or "whoever has my results".
- **Opticians.** Opticians want any contact to carry their own name and no extra manual work. The one ophthalmologist interviewed would add a per-customer interval, but her form has no field for it and risk factors are not collected.
- **Journey.** Today's journey has no step at all after the report for non-referral customers. The legitimate service touchpoints (booking e-mails, the screening itself, the "result ready" e-mail, the report) carry no interval and no consent re-ask.

## 0. Limits of this evidence (read first)

- **Sample size.** n = 4 interviews: 2 customers, 1 optician, 1 ophthalmologist. Plus 15 survey answers to one open question. These are signals, not proportions. No claim below says "most customers" or "most opticians".
- **All synthetic.** Nothing here is a real Mirantus finding.
- **Interviewer notes, not transcripts.** Only text in quotation marks in the notes is treated as a quote. Everything else in the notes is the interviewer's paraphrase. It is labelled "Interviewer note (paraphrase)" and never put in quote marks.
- **Partly translated.**
  - Interview 1: German with verbatim German quotes. The English translations are mine.
  - Interview 3: German. Quotes marked [translated] exist only in the interviewer's English. A few unmarked English phrases ("theoretically", "a few", "A list. Who should come back this month…") were also recorded in English from a German interview, so I treat them as interviewer renderings too.
  - Interview 4: "in German, translated by the interviewer". All its quotes exist only in the interviewer's English.
  - Survey: 11 answers in German, 4 in English. The German-to-English translations are mine.
- **Gaps in coverage.**
  - No mobile-unit voice at all (customer or operator).
  - Only one chain voice: survey answer 7. Customer 02 booked via a chain, but the chain itself is not heard.
  - No customer under 40.
  - No customer with a `minor_findings` result.
  - No customer who did return.
  - Only one ophthalmologist.
  - Survey answers are not labelled by store type.
- **Interpretation** is marked *Interpretation:*. Everything else is attributed to a source.

Quote format: **file** · original wording → English translation.
- "(orig. EN)" means the original was in English.
- "(interviewer EN; no original)" means only the interviewer's translation exists.

## 1. Stakeholders

### 1.1 Customer (Interviews 01, 02)

The two customers are very different:
- **01**: 67, German, long-time customer of an independent optician, unremarkable result.
- **02**: 44, Portuguese in Berlin, found mira via a chain optician's window, referral recommended.

**Goals**
- **Reassurance and convenience, without the barrier of a doctor's appointment.**
  - **02** · "I liked that I didn't need a doctor's appointment. Getting one at an ophthalmologist here takes months." (orig. EN)
  - **01** · "Herr K. hat gesagt, das sollte man in meinem Alter mal machen. Dann hab ich's halt gemacht." → "Mr K. said one should do this at my age. So I just did it."
- **Understandable results in their own language.**
  - **02** wants the report "in English and in normal words" (orig. EN).

**Pains**
- **The report is not understood: jargon.**
  - **01** · "Da standen lauter Fachbegriffe drin. Ich hab nur geguckt, ob irgendwo was Rotes steht. Stand nichts, also war alles gut." → "It was full of technical terms. I only looked whether there was anything red anywhere. There wasn't, so everything was fine."
  - She opened the PDF "kurz" → "briefly".
- **The report is not understood: language.**
  - **02** used Google Translate on the German PDF: "half of it made no sense" (orig. EN).
  - Interviewer note (paraphrase): he wasn't sure how urgent the referral was.
- **An unremarkable result is read as "no need to return".**
  - **01** · "Warum? War doch alles in Ordnung." → "Why? Everything was fine."
- **Weak recognition of mira/Mirantus.**
  - **01** · Interviewer note (paraphrase): she doesn't remember the name Mirantus or mira. She calls it "der Augen-Check bei Herrn K." → "the eye check at Mr K.'s".
  - **02** · "I don't even remember which store it was." (orig. EN)
  - *Interpretation:* the two customers remember different anchors. The independent-store customer remembers the person (her optician). The chain/online customer remembers neither the store nor the brand, only that someone "has my results".
- **Price of repeat use.**
  - **01** · "für einmal okay, aber jedes Jahr? Weiß ich nicht." → "okay for once, but every year? I don't know."
  - **02** · Interviewer note (paraphrase): he would pay again.
- **Consent given, nothing received.**
  - **02** · Interviewer note (paraphrase): he ticked the reminders box at booking and has received nothing since the report.
- **Friction on the referral path.**
  - **02** · Interviewer note (paraphrase): the earliest partner-practice slot was 6 weeks out. The practice then told him to come back in a year for another pressure check.
- **A risk factor that was never captured.**
  - **01** · Interviewer note (paraphrase): her sister has glaucoma ("Grüner Star" → "glaucoma", colloquial German). She didn't mention this at the screening, and nobody asked.

**Channel habits**
- **01** reads e-mail "ab und zu" → "now and then".
- **01** · Interviewer note (paraphrase): she doesn't use apps for health matters, but uses WhatsApp with her grandchildren.
- **02** · "Just WhatsApp me in a year." (orig. EN)

**What would make them act**
- **A prompt from a trusted person.**
  - **01** · "Wenn Herr K. sagt, ich soll wieder kommen, dann komm ich wieder." → "If Mr K. tells me to come back, I'll come back."
- **A reminder from whoever holds the results.**
  - **02** · "Honestly I'd love a reminder. Just WhatsApp me in a year. I will forget otherwise." (orig. EN)
  - **02** · "But not from the optician. I don't even remember which store it was. I'd want it from whoever has my results." (orig. EN)
- *Interpretation:* both customers describe needing an external trigger. Neither describes returning on their own initiative. They differ on who the trigger should come from (see C1).

### 1.2 Optician (Interview 03, survey)

**Goals**
- **Foot traffic and glasses sales.**
  - **03** · "Die Leute kommen rein, und manche kaufen danach eine Brille." → "People come in, and some buy glasses afterwards."
  - **Survey 13** · "Ehrlich gesagt verdienen wir am Screening wenig. Interessant ist es nur, wenn danach eine Brille verkauft wird." → "Honestly, we earn little on the screening. It's only interesting if glasses are sold afterwards."
- **Differentiation from competitors.**
  - **03** · "Und es unterscheidet uns von der Kette nebenan." → "And it sets us apart from the chain next door."
- **Owning the customer relationship.**
  - **03** · [translated] "The customers are mine. If someone writes to them, it's me. I don't want them getting e-mails from a company they've never heard of." (interviewer EN; no original)

**Pains**
- **No memory or system for follow-up.**
  - **03** · Interviewer note (paraphrase): her staff could "theoretically" (interviewer EN) call screening customers after a year.
  - **03** · "aber wer soll sich das merken?" → "but who is supposed to remember that?"
  - **03** · Interviewer note (paraphrase): she has a CRM from her cash-register vendor, and doesn't know whether it can do reminders.
- **No time.**
  - **Survey 9** · "Wir haben keine Zeit, Kunden anzurufen. Es muss automatisch gehen." → "We have no time to call customers. It has to be automatic."
- **Staff turnover breaks capacity.**
  - **03** · Interviewer note (paraphrase): the trained examiner in store 2 left in summer. Screenings there dropped to almost zero for 6 weeks.
  - **Survey 12** · "Better training for our examiners, we lost our trained person and nobody knew how to do it." (orig. EN)
- **No visibility into their own returns.**
  - **03** · Interviewer note (paraphrase): she knows that "a few" (interviewer EN) customers came back, but can't say how many.
- **Fear of being pushy.**
  - **03** · [translated] "Older customers find it pushy if you call them for something they didn't ask for." (interviewer EN; no original)
- **Customers don't link the report to the store.**
  - **Survey 11** · "Die meisten Kunden wissen gar nicht mehr, dass sie bei uns waren, wenn der Befund per Mail kommt. Der Befund sollte von uns kommen." → "Most customers don't even remember they were at our store when the report arrives by e-mail. The report should come from us."

**What would make them act**
- **A list of customers who are due.**
  - **03** · "A list. Who should come back this month. Then my people can call." (interviewer EN; no original)
  - **Survey 3** · "Eine Liste im Dashboard: wer ist fällig. Wir rufen dann selbst an." → "A list in the dashboard: who is due. We'll call them ourselves."
- **Automation under their own brand.**
  - **Survey 1** · "Eine automatische Erinnerung an den Kunden, aber mit unserem Logo." → "An automatic reminder to the customer, but with our logo."
- **An interval stated in the report.**
  - **Survey 6** · "Wenn im Befund steht „Nächstes Screening in 12 Monaten", dann glauben die Leute das auch." → "If the report says 'Next screening in 12 months', people will believe it."

**Chain vs independent vs mobile**
- **Independent (Interview 03).** The owner decides on communication herself, values personal relationships, and has a vendor CRM. Survey answers 3 and 11 also sound like store-level decision-makers, but they are unlabelled.
- **Chain (survey 7).** "Our chain HQ decides about customer communication, not the store. Talk to them." (orig. EN)
  - *Interpretation:* for chains, "with the optician's name on it" may mean the chain brand, decided centrally, not the individual store.
  - Customer 02 (chain) remembers neither the store nor the brand.
- **Mobile unit.** There is no evidence in either the interviews or the survey.
  - *Interpretation:* a mobile unit has no store for a customer to return to. The "returning store visit" logic, and the "my optician" trust anchor, may not exist there. This is untested.

### 1.3 Evaluating ophthalmologist (Interview 04)

All quotes in this section are the interviewer's English translations of a German call. No original wording is available.

**Goals**
- **Medically appropriate return intervals, matched to risk.**
  - Interviewer note (paraphrase) of her general guidance, which she qualifies as "depends on the person":
    - under 40 without risk factors: every 2–3 years;
    - 40 and over: every 1–2 years;
    - family history of glaucoma, diabetes or high myopia: yearly.
  - This is general guidance. Under `constraints.md`, an interval for a specific customer must come from her evaluation.

**Pains**
- **"Unremarkable" is misread.** "An unremarkable result is a snapshot, not a guarantee. Patients read it as 'I'm healthy'."
- **There is nowhere to record an interval.** Interviewer note (paraphrase): her evaluation form has no field for a recommended interval. Her words: "I would fill it in if it were there. It takes five seconds."
- **Risk factors are missing.** Interviewer note (paraphrase): risk factors like family history aren't asked at the screening, so she often can't tell who should come back sooner.
  - *Interpretation:* customer 01's sister with glaucoma is exactly this case.
- **Reports aren't written for patients.** "They are written for colleagues, honestly. Not for patients."
- **Who she worries about.** [translated] "The referral customers I worry about less — they're in the medical system now. It's the 'unremarkable' 60-year-olds who disappear."

**What would make her act**
- An interval field in the evaluation form: "It takes five seconds."

**The boundary she sets**
- "If you scare people, the ones who come are the anxious ones, not the ones at risk."

**Workload**
- Interviewer note (paraphrase): she evaluates around 200 screenings a month, remotely, in the evenings.
- *Interpretation:* any per-screening addition is multiplied by that volume.

**Who may state intervals**
- Per `constraints.md`, a customer-specific interval is a medical statement. It may only come from the evaluating ophthalmologist, in the report.
- Survey answers 8 ("jährlich" → "annually", on a counter display) and 6 ("12 Monaten" → "12 months") show opticians thinking in a blanket yearly interval. That is in tension with her risk-dependent guidance (see C7).

### 5.2 Hypotheses the data analysis can check in `screenings.csv`

For all of them:
- use a fixed return window, with only customers who had the full window;
- treat referral customers separately;
- read results as correlation, not cause.
